Foundry Lab • Research Internship

Literature Review & Gap Analysis on LLM-based Automatic Educational Question Generation

Alvir Shahriar • Research Intern, Foundry Lab

Abstract & Core Focus

A systematic literature review and empirical gap analysis evaluating the state-of-the-art capability of Large Language Models (LLMs) to automatically generate pedagogical assessment instruments. Investigating contextual depth, Bloom's Taxonomy cognitive alignment, hallucination mitigation, and psychometric validity.

Key Research Objectives:
  • Benchmark prompt engineering techniques for multi-choice vs. open-ended conceptual evaluation.
  • Analyze domain adaptation strategies using parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT/LoRA).
  • Develop automated rubric scoring systems to evaluate machine-generated assessment quality.

ENTRY #02: LATE NIGHTS & REASON 2:45 AM Reflections

"I don't write code to show off syntax. I write code because I love taking a blank screen and making something work that didn't exist an hour ago."

At IIUC, during 3rd semester, I spent 48 hours straight debugging a race condition in a relational database engine. People asked why I didn't just use a generic template. But when you build it with your own hands, you own every byte. That obsession with ownership is what drives my work.

At Foundry Lab, exploring LLMs taught me that true AI engineering isn't writing clever prompts — it's constructing rigorous evaluation benchmarks, preventing hallucinations, and holding machines accountable to human cognitive standards.

"Taste is knowing what to subtract. Speed is knowing what not to touch."
ENTRY #03: RULES I LIVE & CODE BY Core Principles
1. Respect the User's Attention. Don't build generic bloated templates with 50 marketing buzzwords. Make the product fast, tactile, and unforgettable.
2. Understand Under the Hood. If you don't understand how your database indexes queries or how your render loop draws frames, you're not engineering — you're guessing.
3. Ship Real Things. Ideas are cheap. Working code with live users (like EduLearn, NextGenNerds, and Foundry Lab papers) is all that speaks.
